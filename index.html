Why Should You Choose an Emergency Dentist in Scarborough?

An emergency dentist can assess sudden tooth pain, dental injuries, swelling, and other urgent oral problems and decide what care is needed. The Canadian Dental Association advises contacting a dentist promptly when a dental emergency occurs.

Dental problems can start with a small symptom and become harder to manage when care is delayed. For example, tooth sensitivity may point to decay, while strong pain can be linked to a damaged or infected tooth. However, only a dental examination can identify the actual cause.

What Counts as a Dental Emergency?

A dental emergency can include severe tooth pain, a broken or knocked-out tooth, swelling, bleeding, or an injury affecting the teeth or mouth. These problems should be assessed by a dental professional rather than treated only at home.

Some common reasons for urgent dental care include:

  • Severe or sudden toothache
  • A chipped, cracked, or broken tooth
  • A knocked-out permanent tooth
  • Facial or gum swelling
  • Dental pain with sensitivity to hot or cold
  • A loose tooth after an injury
  • Bleeding that does not stop
  • Pain around a wisdom tooth
  • A lost filling or restoration causing pain
  • Signs that may indicate a dental infection

The Canadian Dental Association also lists unexplained bleeding, persistent mouth sores, unusual patches, numbness, and ongoing tooth sensitivity among signs that should be checked by a dentist.

Therefore, severe symptoms should not be ignored simply because the pain becomes less intense for a short time. Dental pain can change, but the underlying problem may still need treatment.

How Is Severe Tooth Pain Assessed?

Severe tooth pain needs an examination because several different dental problems can cause similar symptoms.

A cavity can cause sensitivity and pain. A damaged tooth can hurt during chewing. An infected tooth may cause stronger, ongoing pain and swelling. Wisdom teeth can also create pain when there is limited space or inflammation around the area.

The dentist may ask when the pain began, what makes it worse, and whether there is swelling or sensitivity. A dental examination may also be supported by dental X-rays when appropriate.

The Canadian Dental Association notes that dentists are trained to examine oral conditions, identify problems, recommend treatment, and perform emergency procedures when required.

Because pain can have different causes, taking leftover medication or delaying an examination is not a substitute for professional care.

Can a Broken or Damaged Tooth Be Treated?

A broken or damaged tooth should be assessed by a dentist because treatment depends on how much of the tooth has been affected.

A small chip may require different care from a tooth with a deep crack or severe structural damage. If the inner part of the tooth is affected, root canal treatment may sometimes be considered. If the tooth cannot be saved, extraction may be recommended.

Markham Gateway Dentistry offers dental fillings and root canal treatments, along with emergency tooth extraction.

Until the tooth is assessed, avoid chewing hard foods on the affected side. Also, do not try to repair the tooth yourself with household products.

What Should You Do Before Visiting an Emergency Dentist?

Before visiting an emergency dentist, keep the affected area clean and contact the dental office for instructions. The right steps depend on the type of dental problem.

For tooth pain, gentle brushing and regular oral hygiene can help keep the area clean. However, brushing should not replace professional assessment when pain is severe or persistent.

If a tooth has been injured, tell the dental office what happened and when it happened. This information can help the dental team understand the situation.

What Should You Tell the Dental Team?

Give the dental team clear information about your symptoms, medications, allergies, and medical history.

Useful information includes:

  • When the pain or injury started
  • Where the pain is located
  • Whether swelling is present
  • Whether the tooth is broken or loose
  • Whether hot or cold makes the pain worse
  • Any recent dental treatment
  • Current medications
  • Known allergies
  • Relevant medical conditions

The Canadian Dental Association recommends keeping your dentist informed about medications and other health information because these details can affect pain management and treatment decisions.

What Should You Avoid During a Dental Emergency?

Avoid placing aspirin directly on a tooth or gum, using sharp objects to remove something stuck between teeth, or attempting to repair a broken tooth at home.

Do not take more medication than directed on the label or by a dentist or pharmacist. The Canadian Dental Association advises patients to use medications as directed and discuss pain management with their dentist or pharmacist.

If facial swelling is severe or you have trouble breathing or swallowing, seek emergency medical care immediately rather than relying only on a dental appointment.

Why Is Prompt Dental Care Important?

Can Delaying Treatment Make a Dental Problem Worse?

Yes, delaying treatment can allow some dental problems to progress, although the effect depends on the condition.

For example, untreated tooth decay can continue to damage the tooth. A damaged filling can allow food and bacteria to reach areas that are harder to clean. A dental infection can also require prompt care.
